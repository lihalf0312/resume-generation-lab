# Source organization and English writing

Keep original files/text unchanged. For messy materials, group facts by organization, period, project, personal action, output and result; deduplicate repeated passages. A later date alone does not settle a conflict. Explicit withdrawals remain history rather than competing current facts. Record missing versus explicitly removed material separately.

Separate course completion from a degree award; work from training or simulation; personal actions from team results; proposals/pilots from completed deployments; recall estimates from measured results. A job title, a report's existence or an assigned period does not establish who wrote, coordinated, designed or approved it. Natural paraphrase is welcome when it preserves actor, action, object, phase and scope; do not apply verb blacklists. A source gap is not proof of real-world fabrication, and ambiguity may require review.

When a base number conflicts, omit that number and quantities actually derived from it. Do not choose, average or reverse-engineer a preferred value. A directly stated team total compatible with every possible personal quantity can remain unless the source says it was calculated from the disputed quantity. Sharing a project or part/whole relationship alone is not computational dependence. Keep units, population, device/dataset, location, measurement basis and personal/team attribution when material to meaning.

Ask a focused necessary question only when its answer changes current truth or important selection. Optional contacts, portfolio links or irrelevant details should not block a safe draft. Unanswered necessary questions retain `awaiting_user`, null answers and affected facts outside the resume; do not assert the disputed content. Use existing clearly attributed actions in the principal role rather than leaving an empty employer/date shell. Early short roles may be listed briefly.

Select actions and deliverables that support the JD; prioritize relevance and evidence over exhaustive coverage. Missing preferred credentials do not require a negative qualifications section. Preserve employment ownership and dates when combining related evidence. Do not turn a visit into development, checking records into creating records, assisting into independent ownership, testing into formal model training, or a team result into an exclusive personal result. Source-supported leadership can naturally accompany a team result with its proper scope.

Write concise, idiomatic English with concrete verbs and objects. Avoid third-person candidate narration, repetitive paid labels, audit jargon and unnecessary negative disclaimers. Keep internships/volunteering and other material status distinctions. Use consistent descriptive translations of organization names without claiming they are official. Do not add photos, political information, location eligibility, contact details or proficiency levels absent user confirmation. Single-line education/tools information usually omits terminal periods; preserve normal punctuation in prose and internal punctuation in abbreviations/URLs. Keep ordinary ASCII spaces; never remove all spaces to force a match.

# Evidence records

`analysis-first.json` has editor `Codex-assisted semantic editing`, `facts`, `selection`, `questions`, and `claim_map`. Facts have unique `id`, a concise `statement` and `source_fragments`. Each fragment is `{field:"material_text", start:0, end:12, quote:"literal text"}` with exact Unicode code-point offsets `[start,end)` into the original decoded input field. Keep multiple fragments separate; translations, ellipses and stitched quotations are summaries rather than literal quotes. Selection records fact IDs, JD matches, adopted/omitted alternatives and reasons. Questions distinguish necessary/optional and their status. Claim-map records paragraph IDs, actual candidate wording and supporting fact IDs, including names, entries, dates and bullets. Initial paragraph IDs use name, s0.i0 and s0.i0.b0, etc.; section titles and Target are presentation/intent rather than employment claims.

An edit decision uses editor `Codex-assisted semantic editing` and `operations`, each with `paragraph_id`, `action` (`replace` or `remove`), exact `before` (text plus ` | date` for a dated entry), replacement `after` text and/or `after_date` where applicable, `reason`, and `source_fragments`. At least one material fragment is required per edit. JD fragments may additionally explain selection, never substantiate personal facts. A `replace` operation can change text with `after`, an entry's date with `after_date`, or both. At least one is required. `after` and `after_date` must be strings; `after_date` is valid only on an entry row with action `replace`, never on bullets/headings or removal. A date-only operation preserves its existing text. The exact `before` still includes the current displayed date, so stale-date patches fail. Date changes require at least one exact material_text fragment even when JD evidence is also supplied. Full before_row/after_row including dates are logged; this is model-assisted semantic editing, not deterministic inference of a correct year. An empty after_date explicitly omits the date; use it only for an authorized safe omission with a recorded reason. The editor must independently check date meaning, precision, ownership and status against the cited source. Save a fresh external source review after changes, including source hashes, checked claims, gaps and unresolved questions.

Literal citations, schema checks and declared scope comparison do not establish entailment, annotation completeness, natural language quality or truthful self-review. `scope_contract.py` compares declared fields only. `review_rules.py` distinguishes clarification/tail/marker responsibility only. Record factual contradiction, support gap, pending ambiguity, reasonable paraphrase/omission, important evidence omission, expression, job-fit gap, citation integrity and delivery protocol separately. Do not count every observation as a factual error or equate positive comments with error allegations.


# Analysis reference schema (RC2)

`facts`, `selection`, `questions` and `claim_map` must be lists of objects. Every fact `id` is a nonempty unique string. Each selection entry requires `fact_ids`, a nonempty list of existing fact IDs, whether adopted or omitted. Each question requires `affected_fact_ids`, a list of existing fact IDs; it may be empty when asking for new information such as optional contact details. The legacy key `affected_facts` is rejected so a misspelled or migrated reference cannot silently escape checking. Claim-map entries require a nonempty `fact_ids` list of existing IDs. Source fragments retain exact bounded Unicode intervals. Empty selection/questions lists are valid when no such records are needed; the writer still must explain meaningful selections and unresolved issues.

The runtime checks these references before saving the first draft. These checks establish declared ID integrity and literal quotation integrity only. They do not prove that selected facts matter to the JD, that every question or claim was annotated, that questions are necessary, or that candidate wording means the same as its linked facts. After later edits, review the lineage and actual changed text against the sources; structural folding may remove an original paragraph, and the frozen first analysis remains historical rather than a freshly certified final claim map.

Read [independent review/status rules](review-status.md) when recording source review, evaluations, feedback or final acceptance. It defines the independent confirmation/detection/judgment fields and speech acts, including how to preserve original evaluations and corrections.

For current ratings, certificate states and other time-sensitive personal values, record their confirmation/as-of date; a current value is not automatically a lifetime maximum or a live future value. Use the actual supplied tool/course/certificate names. Do not infer proficiency from the JD, substitute dataset coverage dates for missing development dates, or treat missing repetition of an old experience as its withdrawal.


# Activity, responsibility and identifier boundaries (unreleased candidate)

Check these distinctions both before saving the first draft and after every semantic compression. Compare the actual candidate sentence with the complete cited source, not just the claim-map label.

- An activity not performed is not a statement about assigned responsibility. "Did not perform financial analysis during the internship" does not support "Financial analysis was outside the role's responsibilities." Preserve the time/activity limitation when relevant, or omit an irrelevant negative statement. Use a duty-exclusion claim only if a separate source explicitly establishes that scope. Treat an unsupported exclusion as a support gap; do not automatically call it a contradiction.
- Recording, checking or sorting record identifiers does not establish scheduling work, a workflow purpose or an efficiency result. "Logged request IDs" supports a concise description of that logging. "Supported scheduling by logging request IDs" requires evidence that the action actually supported scheduling; "improved scheduling efficiency" additionally requires supported outcome/measurement scope. A plausible purpose from the JD cannot supply this evidence.
- Separately evidenced responsibilities, purposes and outcomes may be retained with their proper actor, period and measurement basis. Do not ban words such as support, scheduling or efficiency. Missing or ambiguous support should be kept distinct from an explicitly contradicted claim. Ask only if the unresolved distinction materially changes the current draft; otherwise use supported wording or safe omission.

For these specific relations, reviewers may call review_rules.semantic_boundary_review with explicitly annotated source_kind, claim_kind and support_state. This helper checks the supplied relation labels only. It neither reads prose nor verifies the support annotation, and is not an automatic runtime/freeze gate. Do not treat a non-flagged declaration as source-truth acceptance.


# Actor and evidence ownership (unreleased candidate extension)

A resume bullet normally asserts an action by the candidate even when it omits "I". Check the actor for each action, not just whether the same nouns or numbers appear in a cited span. Background facts, project constraints and documents with an unspecified author do not establish personal work.

- "The workshop notes estimated demand at around 18 seats" establishes an estimate in notes, not that the candidate recorded, estimated, compiled or maintained those notes. "Recorded demand estimates" requires separate evidence of the candidate's recording action. Preserve the estimate's uncertainty and document attribution if the context is useful; otherwise omit it.
- "The project budget ceiling was 250 credits" establishes a constraint. It does not establish that the candidate kept accounts, tracked expenditure, reconciled costs or managed the budget. State only a supported personal action, or retain the constraint as attributed project context when relevant.
- Where the candidate's authorship or action is explicitly supported, retain it with the actual object, phase and scope. Do not ban recording/accounting verbs or strip evidenced work. Ambiguous authorship stays pending rather than becoming either assumed authorship or an automatic contradiction.

For every selected claim, separately identify the source's actor, the candidate sentence's actor (including implicit resume-bullet attribution), the action, and the evidence connecting that person to that action. Exact citation integrity verifies that words occur in the input; it does not verify this connection. The optional declaration helper can flag annotated background_note/budget_constraint to personal_recordkeeping relations, but a correct label and support state still require contextual judgment.
