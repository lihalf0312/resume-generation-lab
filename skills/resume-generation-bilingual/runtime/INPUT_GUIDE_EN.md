# Preparing information for a bilingual resume

[Back to start](README.md) · [中文](INPUT_GUIDE_ZH.md)

You can start a draft before every detail is available. Share the target role and the experience you can confirm. Mark other details as “to confirm,” “not provided,” or “do not display.” Unknown details and unanswered questions cannot become resume facts. Questions affecting identity, qualifications, dates, or ownership of results need to be resolved before finalizing those claims.

## What to prepare first

1. **Target role and job description**: paste the original JD, choose Chinese, English, or both, and note any experience you want to emphasize. If there is no JD yet, give the target role and say “JD not yet available.” Do not invent one. Job requirements guide relevance; they are not evidence that you have the requested skills.
2. **Education, employment, and projects**: identify the school or organization, degree or role, project, team, and your own work. Distinguish employer, client, organizer, and partner. Be specific about whether you led, contributed, or assisted. A team's total output should not be attributed entirely to you.
3. **Dates and current status**: state the month and year and what happened, such as joining, leaving, project start or end, coursework completion, expected graduation, actual graduation, or degree conferral. These may differ. Keep conflicting dates with their sources and mark them for confirmation. A later record is not automatically a correction; say explicitly which statement replaces the other.
4. **Numbers and results**: include units, scope, period, denominator or comparison baseline, and whether the result belongs to you or the team. For example, “The team collected 120 surveys; I cleaned 80 of them” must not become “I completed 120 surveys.” Explain what “20% growth” measures and the comparison period. Concrete actions and deliverables are useful even without reliable metrics.
5. **Actual grades and skills**: relevant course results, GPA, rank, awards, certificates, languages, and tools can help. Give the grading scale and the population behind a rank. For skills, describe where you used them and what you can do. “Not provided” does not mean “does not have.” Distinguish absent, unknown, and intentionally withheld information. Never invent details to fill the template.
6. **Display information and preferences**: provide the name and English spelling you want, contact details you choose to show, section order, and other layout preferences. Placeholders are fine during testing. Do not supply a full home address, government ID or passport numbers, document scans, account login details, passwords, or verification codes.

## English names are helpful but optional

If you know an official English name for a school, organization, or project, provide the original name, English name, and known source, such as the wording on its website or formal materials. Leave it blank if unknown; an initial draft does not depend on it.

Where no official name is available, you may explicitly approve a transliteration or descriptive translation and label it “non-official.” In the fictional example below, “Campus Survey Project” is an applicant-approved descriptive name only. You may also leave the name pending or explicitly request the original wording. An English draft retaining Chinese names will flag the remaining language question. An official name will not be guessed, and the organization or project's identity will not be removed because its English name is missing.

## How education information is displayed

State whether you are currently enrolled, a recent graduate, already graduated, or in another situation. Dates alone do not establish this. If you are still enrolled and have provided an expected graduation date, it can be shown as “Expected graduation: month year.” For graduates, a separate degree-conferral field or extra graduation-date suffix is omitted by default. Supported study dates may remain. The original dates stay distinct and cannot substitute for one another; an application-specific requirement can override the display default using the matching fact.

Your requested section order comes first. Education normally appears first for current students and recent graduates; work experience may come first when you have substantial relevant experience. A documented educational strength, or your explicit preference based on that strength, can also put education first. School rankings or prestige are not inferred. If status is unclear, the existing order is retained.

## Optional images for the Chinese resume

- **Portrait**: if you want one, provide a local PNG or JPEG and explicitly enable it. It goes in the upper-right corner. This means a resume headshot, not an image of an ID card or passport.
- **School logo**: current students and recent graduates may choose one themselves. State your status, provide the image, and explicitly enable it. It goes in the upper-left corner. School ranking is not a reason to recommend showing or hiding it.
- Either image, both, or neither is fine. Missing or disabled images do not prevent basic generation. Images are used for local layout only and are not sent to a model API. They are not added to the English resume by default. Keep images, encoded image data, and document scans out of the text materials.

## Copyable template

Plain text is enough; no JSON is required. Repeat the experience fields as needed and leave optional items blank.

```text
Target role:
Original JD (or say it is not yet available):
Languages: Chinese / English / both
Name and English spelling (placeholders are fine for testing):
Contact details to display (optional):
Current status: enrolled / recent graduate / graduated / other / to confirm
Education: school, subject, degree or qualification; meaning of each date
Experience: employer or project owner; my role; start and end dates
My own work: action, object, method, or deliverable
Results and numbers: units, period, denominator or baseline; personal / team
Actual grades or skills: grading scale or practical use (optional)
English names: original name; English name; official source or approved non-official wording
Conflicts or questions: both statements, their sources, and any explicit correction
Layout: section order; Chinese portrait on / off; Chinese school logo on / off
Information not to display:
```

## A wholly fictional example

All names, institutions, roles, projects, and figures below are invented to illustrate the format. They are not experience you can claim.

```text
Target role: user research intern. Fictional JD: assist with survey cleaning and summaries.
Languages: both. Name: 林示例 / Lin Shili (fictional). Contact details: do not display.
Education: 澄星示例学院 (fictional), bachelor's program in sociology, currently enrolled.
Started: 2023-09. Expected graduation: 2027-06. Official English school name: unknown, to confirm.
Project: 校园问卷项目, 2026-03 to 2026-05, run by a course team.
My role and work: team member; cleaned 80 survey responses in Excel and prepared a summary sheet.
Ownership: the team collected 120 responses; I processed 80. No verified growth percentage.
Project English name: Campus Survey Project, an applicant-approved non-official description.
GPA: not provided, not evidence of no academic results. Skills: Excel filters and pivot tables used in this project.
Date question: an older record says the project ended in 2026-04; the current record says 2026-05. Neither has been confirmed as a correction.
Layout: education first; portrait off; school logo off.
```

Before starting, check that your facts are separate from the JD, numbers and dates have clear meanings, and open questions are labeled. Other information can follow later.
